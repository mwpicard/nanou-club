Resurgence of a world-cruising sailboat

Twenty years in harbour is long enough.

NANOU is a 35-ton steel cutter, built 1986 in Durban, veteran of two world circumnavigations. She lies today in Port de Masnou, near Barcelona — sound of hull, rich of history, and waiting.

Her first skipper, Bernard, escaped communist Poland around 1970 in a steel ship he built inside a mine, bribing officials to let him run it into the Baltic. He then sailed the world on artful paperwork, carrying backpackers from continent to continent for a few dollars a day. When NANOU was chained to a San Francisco dock in 1988, an old salt met a new sailor — and after a boat swap that spans three oceans and one very long story, NANOU ended up in Barcelona.

The full tale is available in exchange for cerveza. Twenty years of the earlier chapters — the Maldives, the Red Sea, Suez, house arrest in Ulgamu — live on at nanou.org.

The club

The club exists to get her sailing again. Members fund the recommissioning and running costs — published openly, with fees decided by club vote. Paid crew keep her seaworthy. In return, members join voyages:
